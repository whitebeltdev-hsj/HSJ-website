01 The missing takedown

Control starts
standing up.

Most grapplers can play guard and pass, then freeze the moment the match starts on the feet. Takedowns are where control begins, and they are learnable. You'll start with a solid stance and clean motion, then layer in level changes and the core shots step by step, until getting to the legs feels natural. Every bit of it feeds directly into your Jiu Jitsu, because the person who scores the takedown decides where the fight goes.

  • Stance & motionThe base everything else is built on, drilled until it's second nature.
  • Level changes & shotsSingle legs, double legs, and the setups that open them up.
  • Finishes & controlCompleting the takedown and keeping top position once you're there.

Wrestling, answered.

I have never wrestled. Can I start?
Yes. The fundamentals are built for beginners, taught slowly and clearly, so you can pick up stance, motion, and the core shots from scratch. Your first three classes are free, so there's no commitment to find out if it's for you.
I wrestled years ago. Is it for me too?
Yes. Returning wrestlers are welcome, and the room has high-level partners who will help you knock the rust off and get sharp again. You'll find people to push you whatever your starting point.
Do I need to be a Jiu Jitsu student?
No. You can train wrestling on its own. That said, wrestling and Jiu Jitsu feed each other, and many of our students train both because the takedown changes everything about how a roll starts.
What does it cost?
Membership is month-to-month or annual, and your first three classes are free. Every price, including family rates and the freeze policy, is on the pricing page.
